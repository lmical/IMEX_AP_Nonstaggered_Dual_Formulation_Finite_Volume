--------------------------------------------------------------------

Possible paragraph to insert in the Introduction
--------------------------------------------------------------------

\RIIIcolor{
Semi-implicit and semi-Lagrangian ideas have a long history in numerical weather prediction and geophysical fluid dynamics, especially for shallow-water, primitive-equation, and all-scale atmospheric models. Classical contributions include the analysis of gravity-wave dynamics and nonlinear normal-mode initialization in shallow-water models~\cite{Machenhauer_1977}, two-time-level semi-Lagrangian semi-implicit schemes~\cite{Temperton_Staniforth_1987}, semi-Lagrangian spectral shallow-water models~\cite{Ritchie_1988}, and the review of semi-Lagrangian methods for atmospheric models~\cite{Staniforth_Cote_1991}. Related all-speed and low-Mach-number semi-implicit ideas were also developed in the context of Godunov-type schemes~\cite{Klein_1995}. More recent developments include finite-volume all-scale atmospheric models, such as the ECMWF finite-volume module~\cite{Smolarkiewicz_2016}, mimetic semi-implicit finite-volume shallow-water discretizations~\cite{Thuburn_Cotter_Dubos_2014}, compatible finite-element methods for geophysical fluid dynamics~\cite{Cotter_2023}, and C-grid/mimetic discretizations on polygonal or non-orthogonal spherical grids~\cite{Weller_2014}. Since the semi-implicit update in the present method leads to elliptic problems, we also mention the classical projection and MAC approaches~\cite{Harlow_Welch_1965,Chorin_1968}. The present work differs from these approaches in that it focuses on an AP finite-volume dual-formulation method for the TRSW equations, combining a primitive-variable discretization tailored to the TQG low-Rossby-number limit with a conservative shock-capturing discretization for high- and intermediate-Rossby-number regimes.
}

{\sc\underline{Reply}}:
We thank the reviewer for this important comment. We agree that the original manuscript did not sufficiently place the proposed method within the broader literature on semi-implicit schemes for numerical weather prediction and geophysical fluid dynamics. We have therefore expanded the Introduction by adding references to classical semi-implicit and semi-Lagrangian approaches for atmospheric and shallow-water models, including the works of Machenhauer~\cite{Machenhauer_1977}, Temperton and Staniforth~\cite{Temperton_Staniforth_1987}, Ritchie~\cite{Ritchie_1988}, and Staniforth and C{\^o}t{\'e}~\cite{Staniforth_Cote_1991}. We have also added references to related all-speed and low-Mach-number semi-implicit ideas~\cite{Klein_1995}, to finite-volume all-scale atmospheric models such as the ECMWF finite-volume module~\cite{Smolarkiewicz_2016}, to mimetic semi-implicit finite-volume shallow-water discretizations~\cite{Thuburn_Cotter_Dubos_2014}, to compatible finite-element methods for geophysical fluid dynamics~\cite{Cotter_2023}, and to C-grid/mimetic discretizations on polygonal or non-orthogonal spherical grids~\cite{Weller_2014}. Since our semi-implicit update involves the solution of elliptic problems, we have also added references to classical projection and MAC-type methods~\cite{Harlow_Welch_1965,Chorin_1968}.

We have also clarified the positioning of the present work with respect to this literature. Our goal is not to propose an operational NWP solver, but rather to develop an AP finite-volume dual-formulation method for the TRSW equations. The method combines a primitive-variable discretization designed to recover the TQG low-Rossby-number limit with a conservative shock-capturing discretization needed away from that limit. This additional discussion is intended to make the methodological rationale and the relation to existing semi-implicit approaches clearer.
